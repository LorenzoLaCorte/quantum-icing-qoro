THE PROBLEM

    
Wire 23 nodes so no link drowns.

  


  

    
01

    
Twenty-three nodes.

    
Each one is a site that sends traffic to every other site. The bigger the glow, the more traffic it handles.

  


  

    
02

    
506 flows of traffic.

    
Every ordered pair has a demand, from tiny to huge: 23 × 22 = 506 flows that all have to arrive.
      Each line here is one pair, drawn brighter where the demand is larger.

  


  

    
03

    
Two links out. Two links in.

    
Every node gets exactly two outgoing and two incoming one-way links, 46 in total.
      Which 46 links to build is the entire problem: one binary variable per possible link, 506 of them.

  


  

    
04

    
The score is the busiest link.

    
All demand is routed optimally over the links with an exact multicommodity-flow LP. The objective is the load on the
      most congested link, and lower is better. This network scores 639,739.

  


  

    
05

    
6.07 × 1042 ways to wire it.

    
We counted them exactly: 6,066,885,060,026,725,326,590,776,684,425,359,774,363,091 valid networks.
      Checking a billion every second since the Big Bang would cover less than one in 1016 of them.

ACT I · INHERIT

    
Start from something good.

  


  

    
06

    
A 24-node network we had already solved.

    
Our network24 hand-in scores 657,121, 0.99% below its best known value.
      Parallel tempering, then iterated local search, then parallel tempering again.

  


  

    
07

    
Delete node 24. Splice its wires.

    
Node 24's two incoming links are joined to its two outgoing ones. The result is a valid 23-node network that scores
      642,084, already 2.62% below network23's best known value.

  


  

    
08

    
Two exact swaps.

    
A 2-exchange swaps the heads of two links and keeps every degree intact. An exact-LP descent finds two that help:
      642,084 → 640,472 → 639,739.

  


  

    
09

    
Stuck, and we can prove it.

    
Every network that differs from this one in two or three links was enumerated and routed, and none of them is better.
      This is a certified radius-3 local optimum. To escape it, a move has to rewire more links than an exhaustive search
      can afford to try. That is where Grover comes in.

THE FINE PRINT

  
What is quantum here, and what isn't.

  

    

      
SIMULATED

      
Grover runs as an ideal statevector simulation, with a phase oracle that marks a threshold computed classically. A gate-level Qiskit simulation of the first improving round reproduced the probabilities to within 1.3 × 10⁻¹³.

    

    

      
CIRCUIT COST

      
The 11-qubit round transpiles to 17,164 CNOTs on rz · sx · x · cx, before any hardware mapping. That is out of reach of today's devices, and we say so.

    

    

      
SAME BUDGET

      
Grover, greedy and uniform selection each get the same 120 exact LP evaluations per round. Only Grover's picks drive the walk. Over 364 rounds on 7 instances: 294 / 56 / 14 against uniform, 4 / 309 / 51 against greedy (wins / ties / losses).

    

    

      
CHECKED

      
Every score on this page comes from a .sol file with integer flows that passes QOBLIB's upstream Rust checker. The replay above runs the original code with seed 73 and lands on the same file.
